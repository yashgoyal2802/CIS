# CIS Scope B charter (working draft)

Working title: **AI across Indian retail and MSME lending: what is deployed, what is disclosed, and where it could go next (servicing, collections, underwriting)**

Status: draft for the student to edit and for faculty mentor review. Evidence references (A/B/C/X grades, source IDs S01-S52) are in `CIS workflow map and evidence base.md`.

## 1. Problem statement

Indian banks and NBFCs describe AI as central to lending, and the regulator has published a framework (RBI FREE-AI, Aug 2025) and a sector survey. But public evidence of what AI actually does at each stage of the lending workflow, and with what result, is uneven. Customer service has repeated, numeric disclosure (for example Bajaj Finance's quarterly self-service series). Collections and early warning show activity counts or none, and no bank in the documents reviewed discloses contact rates, roll-back rates, cost to collect, or early-warning hit rates or lead times. Claims in the press about outcomes (such as SBI's early-warning and disbursal-time claims) could not be found in the lenders' own documents.

This leaves a decision problem. A lender, regulator or student of the sector cannot tell which parts of the lending workflow have proven AI value, which have only activity, and which are unknown. The project maps that, then proposes where AI could improve each stage and what evidence would be needed to prove it.

## 2. Objectives

1. Map the end-to-end Indian retail and MSME lending workflow into stages and sub-steps, and show how the process runs today.
2. For servicing, collections and underwriting, document what AI is used for, by whom, and how much, using only verified sources, each tagged with its evidence grade.
3. For early warning, document what the regulator requires against what lenders disclose, as a named evidence gap.
4. Identify, stage by stage, where AI is absent, partial or unmeasured, separating "not adopted" from "not disclosed".
5. Propose three AI improvements as testable hypotheses (what, why, data needed, risk, how to measure), checked against the regulatory frame.

Objective links: 1 feeds 2 and 3, which feed 4, which feeds 5. Objective 5 is only as strong as the evidence base in 2-4.

## 3. Research questions

- RQ1. At each of the three focus stages, which AI uses at Indian lenders are documented, and at what scale?
- RQ2. Where are outcomes (cost, quality, recovery, risk) disclosed, and where only activity?
- RQ3. What do RBI rules require for early warning and AI governance, and how does lender disclosure compare?
- RQ4. Which gaps are real adoption gaps, and which are disclosure gaps?
- RQ5. Which three improvements are most defensible given the evidence and the regulation?

## 4. Scope

In scope: retail and MSME lending by Indian banks and NBFCs; servicing, collections, underwriting (lighter); early warning as a gap; the regulatory frame (FREE-AI, Digital Lending Directions, Fraud Risk Management Directions, KYC, DPDP).

Out of scope: acquisition, KYC technology detail, payments-side fraud (MuleHunter), AML, corporate lending, vendor product comparisons, building a model.

Named comparators: Bajaj Finance (deepest evidence, tech-led NBFC, not representative), HDFC Bank, Axis Bank, SBI, ICICI Bank. Optional global comparator: Upstart (US), labelled as company-defined.

## 5. Method

- **Design:** desk-based empirical study (document analysis), consistent with the outline's "empirical exercise". It is a structured evidence review plus a gap analysis, not a statistical test.
- **Sources:** primary documents only for any figure (annual reports, investor decks, earnings-call transcripts, regulator directions and reports). Press and consulting sources are used only to locate leads and are labelled.
- **Evidence grading:** A (primary, read in the original), B (primary, seen via summary), C (press, vendor, consulting), X (unverified or contradicted, not cited). Only A and B figures appear in analysis.
- **Disclosure matrix:** bank-by-stage grid recording whether each of activity, scale, cost, outcome and lead time is disclosed. This is the main analytical tool for RQ2 and RQ4.
- **Gap test:** a gap is called an adoption gap only if a source shows the capability absent. Otherwise it is a disclosure gap.
- **Proposals:** each as a hypothesis with data requirement, risk, regulatory fit and a measurement plan.
- **Verification rule:** before a figure enters the paper, the student opens the original and records document, date and page.

## 7. Limitations to state in the paper

- Almost all quantified AI outcomes are company self-reports, not audited.
- Bajaj Finance is not representative of banks.
- Disclosure absence does not prove absence of impact or adoption.
- Documents are 2026 filings and may be revised.
- Some regulatory texts were read via extraction and need hand checks (see evidence base Section 7).
- No causal claims are made.
