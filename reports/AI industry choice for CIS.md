# Retail banking and lending wins the CIS comparison

**Choose retail banking and lending, narrowed to credit-risk monitoring and collections in Indian retail and MSME lending, with an RBI FREE-AI and Digital Lending compliance lens.** On the re-scored weighted scorecard it earns about **81/100, against 68 for insurance claims and 63 for e-commerce**. Banking wins for three reasons. It is the only industry where an Indian company publishes recurring, numeric AI outcomes (Bajaj Finance gives them quarterly). Its workflow splits cleanly into nine stages, and the regulator's own survey gives a rough stage-by-stage adoption split. It also has a documented, quantified gap in credit-side fraud and early warning, where the rupee losses sit. Insurance claims is a credible runner-up because it has the best official outcome data (IRDAI, NHA) and one audited primary source (the Lemonade 10-K). Its Indian AI evidence is mostly targets and press summaries, though, and primary-source extraction is a real time risk. E-commerce has the most intuitive workflow and the best-known India stories, but almost every quantified result is self-reported, press-relayed, or vendor-sourced. The evidence under all three industries is weaker than the headline numbers suggest. Most Indian figures reached the research notes through press summaries, several primary pages returned HTTP 403 or could not be read, and many figures are explicitly flagged unverified or do-not-cite. This report carries every one of those flags forward, and a verification list sits in the final sections. The choice is a judgement made on the available evidence, not a proven result.

## How the comparison was built and what to trust

The three research notes cover roughly 17 to 24 searches or fetches each, and each tags sources as primary, secondary, vendor or analyst. Three limits apply to everything below. Quantified AI outcomes in all three industries are overwhelmingly self-reported by the firm and relayed by press, so none has been independently audited. Several key primary documents were never opened: the RBI FREE-AI report, IRDAI annual-report PDFs, Business Standard pages (403) and Swiss Re's page (403). The "candidate gaps" in every industry are the notes' own hypotheses, not findings, and no source directly proves under-use. The most useful distinction for the project is between evidence that is primary and read (few items), primary but seen only through a summary (many), and secondary or vendor claims (most).

## E-commerce: best-known stories, weakest evidence

### Workflow and evidence

The notes decompose e-commerce into 12 stages: seller and catalog management, demand generation, discovery, personalisation, pricing and promotions, checkout and risk, planning, fulfilment, last-mile, returns, support, and a cross-cutting layer. This is the researcher's own framework and not a sourced taxonomy. It is broad, which helps for mapping and hurts for an 80-hour scope. The best-documented stages are conversational discovery, warehouse robotics and customer support. Amazon says more than **250M shoppers used Rufus in 2025** and that Rufus users are **60% more likely to complete a purchase**, with a projected **$10B incremental annual sales**. That figure uses company-defined "downstream" seven-day attribution and is not causal ([Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2025/11/02/amazon-rufus-ai-shopping-assistant-chatbot-10-billion-sales-monetization)). Amazon reports its 1 millionth robot across 300+ facilities and expects DeepFleet to improve fleet travel time by **10%**, which is forward-looking ([Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/operations/amazon-million-robots-ai-foundation-model)). The strongest Indian case is Meesho's voice bot in November 2024: about **60,000 calls a day, 95% bot resolution, 50% lower handling time, 10% higher CSAT, and up to 75% lower cost on some call types**, all company self-reported through [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/26/ai-helps-indias-meesho-cut-customer-call-costs-by-75).

### Thin areas, flagged items and gaps

Pricing, demand forecasting, returns, last-mile routing and marketing have almost no published before-and-after numbers for Indian players. Several items are flagged. The Myntra figures (size engine on about 85% of eligible catalog, conversion up about 20%, seller onboarding under two days) and the Flipkart returns-engine, X-ray scanning, "sixfold" AI investment and CX Copilot items come only from search snippets after Business Standard returned 403. They are **UNVERIFIED-SNIPPET** ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/companies/start-ups/myntra-deploys-ai-at-scale-as-rivals-intensify-fashion-ecommerce-push-126072201436_1.html); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/flipkart-plans-ai-powered-facelift-ahead-of-festive-sale/)). The Amazon robotics "$4B savings, $10B by 2030" figure is **UNVERIFIED and not to be used**. A "15% cost reduction, 10% revenue growth" figure is **UNVERIFIED**. The claim that Meesho's fraud system uses GenAI and graph neural networks is aggregator-sourced and should be checked in the RHP. The Rufus 140% versus 149% user-growth conflict is resolved in the notes in favour of the Fortune figure. The Canopy Management "22% overlap" figure on Rufus recommendations is vendor-sourced and unverified. The headline RTO benchmarks (31.6% national fashion RTO in 2026, COD about 24% versus about 10% prepaid, prepaid conversion cutting return probability from 20.9% to 5.8%, Rs 150-300 direct cost per RTO parcel) are aggregated from logistics-vendor blogs with unclear methodology and are order-of-magnitude only ([First Resort](https://www.firstresort.in/blogs/research/fashion-ecommerce-returns-rto-india-2026)). Meesho's contribution margin improvement of 200 bps to 4.9% is not attributable to AI alone.

The candidate gaps are COD and RTO prediction with prepaid nudging for small sellers, returns (fit coverage, return-reason NLP, grading and resale routing, return fraud), vernacular seller tooling for ONDC micro-merchants, tier-3 address and ETA quality, and a measurement gap. The notes label these hypotheses, and no source quantified SME-seller AI adoption in India. Address quality (Gap D) had no source retrieved at all.

### India relevance, regulation and feasibility

India relevance is high. Meesho reports about 80% of customers in smaller cities and towns and 29-31% of FY25 e-commerce shipments, per its issuer-commissioned Redseer report ([Meesho IR](https://investor.meesho.com/investor-web/_next/docs/ipo/industry-report.pdf)). ONDC had about 206,000 transacting merchants by 31 December 2025, per a secondary source ([Unicommerce](https://unicommerce.com/blog/what-is-ondc/)). Nykaa and Amazon India fulfilment AI returned nothing. Regulation constrains exactly the ideas the gaps suggest. The DPDP Rules were notified 14 November 2025 with substantive duties from 13 May 2027, per law-firm blogs and not the MeitY text ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1775140/from-draft-to-reality-key-changes-in-indias-dpdp-rules-2025)). The CCPA dark-pattern guidelines list 13 patterns, and the platform self-declaration count is unresolved (18 versus 26) ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/11/223-ccpa-dark-pattern-self-audit-declarations-18-e-commerce-quick-commerce-platforms/); [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/flipkart-zomato-24-other-ecom-platforms-declare-themselves-free-from-dark-patterns)). Whether the advisory is binding is debated ([Law School Policy Review](https://lawschoolpolicyreview.com/2025/07/11/decoding-the-ccpas-dark-patterns-advisory-binding-guidelines-or-just-soft-law/)). The Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules 2020 were not researched.

The suggested sub-scope is returns and reverse logistics plus COD/RTO risk (stages 6, 9, 10) in Indian fashion and marketplace e-commerce, with Amazon, Walmart and Zalando as global comparators. The notes' estimated budget is 15 h mapping, 20 h evidence and source grading, 15 h gap analysis with an RTO unit-economics model, 15 h proposal design, 10 h writing and 5 h buffer. This is feasible, but the model would rest on vendor ranges treated as stated assumptions, no returns dataset has been confirmed, and the AI-use evidence for exactly this sub-scope is the thinnest in the industry (Flipkart outcomes undisclosed, no return-reduction percentage found).

## Retail banking and lending: the most numbers, the clearest stage map

### Workflow and evidence

The notes split retail lending into nine stages: acquisition and marketing, onboarding and KYC (V-CIP), underwriting and scoring, fraud detection, sanction and disbursal, servicing, collections, portfolio monitoring and early warning, and compliance and AML. The RBI supervisory survey of 612 entities found **583 AI applications**, of which roughly **15.6% were customer support, 11.8% sales and marketing and 13.7% credit underwriting**, and only **20.8%** of entities were deploying or developing AI. Those percentages came through law-firm and press summaries and need checking in the RBI report itself ([Khaitan](https://www.khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2025-08/Ergo%20-%20FREE%20AI%20Framework%20-%2028%20Augusut%202025.pdf); [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/09/223-rbi-warns-on-ai-in-banking-sector-and-opportunities/)).

The best-documented stages are servicing, sales and collections at Bajaj Finance, because management gives quarterly numbers. In Q1 FY27 Bajaj reported bots handling **71% of DIY customer-service volumes**, **Rs 2,500 crore** of bot-led disbursements in the quarter, voice bots contributing **17-18%** of loan originations, and voice AI costing about one-third of a human agent ([MediaNama, Aug 2026](https://www.medianama.com/2026/08/223-bajaj-finances-71-customer-service-2500-crores-disbursements/)). In Q4 FY26 it reported 72% self-service and Rs 1,895 crore of bot-generated disbursals. It also **cut its autonomous-agent target from 800+ to 600+ and dropped sales from scope**, which is useful evidence that agentic scale-up is hard ([MediaNama, May 2026](https://www.medianama.com/2026/05/223-q4fy26-bajaj-finance-autonomous-agent-target-800-600-fy27/)). Both are management earnings-call commentary relayed by press. Axis Bank's investor presentation reports an AI operating model and a GenAI staff chatbot used by 87,000 employees, and **77%** of unsecured business-loan disbursements were end-to-end digital, which is digital lending and not necessarily AI-underwritten ([Axis Q4FY26](https://www.axis.bank.in/docs/default-source/shareholders/financial-results-and-other-information/investor-presentations/2025---2026/investor-presentation-for-the-quarter-ended-31st-march-2026.pdf)). The Account Aggregator rail, which supplies the data layer for AI underwriting, moved about **Rs 3.82 lakh crore across 3.68 crore loans** in FY26, with 18.2% of originations to new-to-credit borrowers. Sahamati is an industry body, and AA is a data rail and not AI itself ([IANS](https://ianslive.in/indias-account-aggregator-ecosystem-drives-rs-382-lakh-crore-loans-in-fy26--20260827133606)). The strongest underwriting numbers are from the US: Upstart reports **91% of loans fully automated** in 2025 and 41.4% more approvals at 33.1% lower APRs, on company-defined terms, with a conflicting snippet (44% and 16%) ([Upstart](https://ir.upstart.com/static-files/77673faa-918f-48b8-a6e2-d30846f896f2)). On the regulator side, RBI's annual report shows **23,953 fraud cases and Rs 36,014 crore**, with over 92% of value in advances ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/bank-fraud-amount-triples-in-fy25-despite-drop-in-number-of-cases-rbi-125052900696_1.html)). MuleHunter.AI had been implemented by 23 banks by December 2025, and Canara's "95% accuracy" is an unaudited bank claim ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/12/223-rti-23-banks-mulehunter-mule-accounts/)). Consultancy figures such as McKinsey's credit-analyst productivity gain of 20-60% and collections cost cuts of up to 40% are estimates and not realised results ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/risk-and-resilience/our-insights/the-promise-of-generative-ai-for-credit-customer-assistance)).

### Thin areas, flagged items and gaps

Large banks describe use cases and rarely give outcomes. HDFC reports a roughly 50% reduction in eligibility-search effort for home-loan pre-approvals and five use cases in production ([Financial Express B2B](https://www.financialexpressb2b.com/futech/features/from-22000-crore-in-ai-led-business-to-faster-loan-journeys-how-ai-is-paying-off-for-sbi-hdfc-bank-and-icici-bank-12392577)). ICICI gives no headline AI number. SBI's Rs 22,000 crore of AI-generated leads is press-reported. SBI's "AI underwrote nearly Rs 1 trillion of MSME loans" is a Business Standard headline whose page returned 403. The notes found **no verified quantified impact of AI underwriting at any Indian bank from a primary source**, nothing on early-warning hit rates, no national V-CIP drop-off data and no Indian AML false-positive data.

Flagged as unverified or do-not-cite: SBI's claims of over 60% of personal loans under Rs 10 lakh disbursed through a pre-approved digital channel in under 4 hours, and MSME early-warning flags up to 90 days ahead (search summary, no clear source); "AI scoring improves default prediction 15-25%, reduces defaults up to 30% in India" (no traceable source); "65% of FIs deployed AI in credit scoring by 2025 (Accenture)" (aggregator only); the Trak.in viral claim that 10 bots replaced about 1,500 agents and cut cost about 70%, which conflicts with Bajaj's one-third cost figure; Bajaj's Rs 5,520 crore FY26 personal-loan figure and the 30 bp opex-to-NII improvement (search summary, and not attributable to AI); the KYC/V-CIP and deepfake-detection requirements sourced from a vendor blog; and Paytm's two-minute AI approval, which is marketing. The Bajaj figures are the most useful but still reach us through press (MediaNama), not transcripts the notes read.

Candidate gaps, all hypotheses, are credit-side fraud and early warning, explainability and fairness operations, AA-plus-AI underwriting for secured and new-to-credit borrowers, vernacular and soft-bucket collections, and smaller lenders. The survey evidence is the strongest gap evidence in any industry: of 127 AI-using entities, only **15% used interpretation tools or audit logs and 35% validated for bias and fairness**, and 38% preferred simple rule-based models for explainability ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/08/223-rbi-committee-sector-specific-models-ai/)). Fraud value sits in loans (over 92%), while published AI fraud work centres on payments and mules. This is an inference, but it rests on two official-source datapoints.

### India relevance, regulation and feasibility

India relevance and recruiter appeal are the highest of the three: UPI, AA and V-CIP form distinctive public infrastructure, and Bajaj, HDFC, Axis, SBI and ICICI are visible employers. The rulebook is dense. RBI's FREE-AI report (13 August 2025; 7 sutras, 26 recommendations) is a committee recommendation and not binding by the notes' own reading, and the PDF could not be fetched ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/in/en/insights/2025/08/rbi-free-ai-committee-report-on-framework-for-responsible-and-ethical-enablement-of-artificial-intelligence.html)). The RBI Digital Lending Directions 2025 make regulated entities fully responsible for their lending-service providers' actions, per law-firm summaries ([Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/finance-and-banking/reserve-bank-of-india-digital-lending-directions-2025)). DPDP duties land in May 2027 (primary: [PIB](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)). RBI's Financial Stability Report flags third-party concentration, herding and deepfake risk.

The notes recommend Option A, credit-risk monitoring and collections, anchored on Bajaj, SBI and Axis, with an alternative of AA-enabled underwriting for new-to-credit and secured lending, which has strong public data but a weaker AI link. The suggested budget is 15 h stage map and source audit, 30 h deep dive on two stages, 15 h gap analysis with three proposals, 10 h regulatory screen and 10 h write-up. Feasibility is good because Bajaj's quarterly commentary and filings are public. The risks are that the SBI early-warning claims are unverified (they anchor the early-warning half), that transcripts and paywalled reports were not time-tested, and that Bajaj is an unusually tech-led NBFC and not representative of banks.

## Insurance claims: best official outcome data, thinnest India AI proof

### Workflow and evidence

The notes map the insurance chain as distribution, underwriting and pricing, issuance, servicing, claims (first notice of loss, triage, documentation, assessment, fraud, settlement, subrogation, grievance) and renewals. The research concentrated on claims. **Distribution, underwriting, servicing and renewals were not researched**, so the "whole workflow" view is incomplete by design. Within claims, evidence is relatively deep. Lemonade's FY2025 10-K reports about **55% of claims automated** and its AI takes first notice of loss with no human **96%** of the time, but "automated" is Lemonade's definition and its book is mostly simple renters, home and pet claims ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1691421/000169142126000016/lmnd-20251231.htm)). Allianz's Project Nemo cut processing time for small food-spoilage claims by about **80%** using seven agents, with a human still making the payout decision, in one product line in Australia ([Allianz](https://www.allianz.com/en/mediacenter/news/articles/251103-when-the-storm-clears-so-should-the-claim-queue.html)). BCG (2025) finds only **7%** of insurers have scaled AI, with about 70% of scaling challenges being people and process, and service-staff productivity gains above 30% ([BCG](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/insurance-leads-ai-adoption-now-time-to-scale)). Those two are the notes' only items cleared as safe to cite as primary or near-primary.

Indian evidence is weaker. Star Health says about **20% of claims are processed via AI** and targets over 50% of routine cashless claims within two years, which is a target relayed by news, with "AI-processed" undefined ([Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/market-updates/star-health-expects-most-cashless-claims-to-be-settled-by-ai-within-2-years)). ICICI Lombard and Bajaj Allianz (30-minute small motor claims) claims are vendor or company marketing with no AI-attributed share. The NHA reports for FY2025-26 that **Rs 678.47 crore of suspicious claims were blocked before payment**, 2,842 hospitals were penalised and 100+ algorithms were used. That is the best Indian public AI-fraud datapoint, but it comes through a trade site citing the NHA annual report ([Medical Dialogues](https://medicaldialogues.in/news/health/hospital-diagnostics/ayushman-bharat-fraud-national-health-authority-slaps-rs-114-crore-penalty-on-2842-hospitals-175384)). Official outcome data that frames the problem: IRDAI Annual Report 2024-25 figures for health (3.26 crore claims, Rs 94,248 crore paid, **8% repudiated**, about 58% cashless) and **2,57,790 grievances** with about 69% of general and health complaints claims-related. These were seen only through an aggregator summary, with a Rs 94,247 versus 94,248 discrepancy ([Algates](https://algatesinsurance.in/irdai-annual-report-2024-25-highlights/)).

### Thin areas, flagged items and gaps

Thin or undocumented areas are subrogation, grievance handling, leakage analytics, reinsurance recovery, and any measured Indian outcome. Flagged items to carry forward: BCG claims-cost reductions up to 20%, claim-speed gains up to 50%, and real-time resolution of up to 70% of simple claims (relayed by a search summary, not verified on the BCG page); "over 90% of claims events processed without automation" (untraceable); McKinsey "34% adoption (up from 8%)", cycle time "30 to 7.5 days" and cost per claim "$40-60 to $25-36" (aggregator only, **do not use**); Ping An's 60% first-time pass improvement and 3.65 bn model calls (aggregator, check the results deck); Zurich's "an hour to seconds" (not opened); Swiss Re ClaimsGenAI (403, no figures); the 15% health-fraud rule of thumb and Rs 600-800 crore of annual losses (no primary source); Star Health's Rs 120 to 200 crore tech spend and two-hour approval (search summary); the EU AI Act 2 August 2026 date (possible delay); the IRDAI AI working group order date (17 versus 19 June 2026); Tokio Marine UAE and SHIFT items (vendor-linked; different entities from the Japanese parent); and the DPDP details, which the insurance note never retrieved. The AXA, Progressive and HDFC Ergo checks produced nothing quantified.

Candidate gaps, all hypotheses, are explainable repudiation review, hospital-side tariff anomaly detection in private health claims, small-ticket straight-through processing on NHCX data, grievance and ombudsman triage (no AI examples found), subrogation and motor third-party recovery (no AI evidence anywhere in this pass), and vernacular voice-first first notice of loss. "No examples found" supports a gap only weakly, since an absence in a 22-call search is not an absence in the market.

### India relevance, regulation and feasibility

Relevance is strong for health claims: high cashless share, 8% repudiation, large public-scheme volume, and new rails (NHCX with 34 insurers and TPAs and 300+ hospitals as of July 2024, current adoption unknown; Bima Sugam status unverified) ([Digital Health News](https://www.digitalhealthnews.com/irdai-plans-to-onboard-health-exchange-and-bima-sugam-by-august-1)). Regulation is in motion. The IRDAI working group on AI (constituted June 2026, three-month mandate, explicit claims and fraud focus) was due to report around September 2026, and the notes did not check whether it has ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/asia/news/technology/indias-insurance-regulator-steps-in-to-govern-ai-adoption-579846.aspx)). The IRDAI Fraud Monitoring Framework took effect on 1 April 2026 ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/irdai-insurance-fraud-monitoring-framework-guidelines-2025.html)). Claims and fraud AI sits outside the EU high-risk list in the notes' reading, unlike pricing, which is an inference.

The notes' narrowed scope is Indian health claims, centred on cashless pre-authorisation, rejection and grievance, at about 15 h mapping, 25 h evidence table, 15 h India gap analysis across 4-5 insurers, 15 h proposal sizing and 10 h risk and writing. Feasibility is moderate. Official data is rich, but the notes name primary-source extraction (IRDAI PDFs, insurer disclosures) as the main time risk, no proprietary claims data is available to a solo student, and impact sizing would be scenario-based.

The ranking survives reasonable pushback. Take one point off banking on any single criterion (at most 5 points off the total) and add one point to insurance on any single criterion (at most 5 points on): banking is still ahead at 76 versus 73. If both banking's data and quantifiable-impact scores each lose a point and insurance gains a point on each, the gap closes to a near tie (73 versus 76). That is why verification of the Bajaj and RBI figures is the first next step, because the recommendation depends on them being what the press summaries say. E-commerce loses mainly on data credibility (2), and even a generous 4 there would put it at 73, below banking.

## Recommendation and next steps

Select retail banking and lending, with the sub-scope "AI in credit-risk monitoring and collections for Indian retail and MSME lending," using Bajaj Finance as the primary case and Axis, SBI, HDFC and ICICI as comparators. Frame the gap hypotheses (credit-side early warning and loan-fraud detection, explainability and fairness operations, vernacular and soft-bucket collections) as hypotheses with evidence tags. Keep the project honest: label every KPI with source type and year, and use consultancy figures only as labelled estimates. The e-commerce returns and RTO scope is the fallback if bank transcripts prove inaccessible, since its sources are public but weak. Health-claims insurance is the fallback if the student prefers official data over company disclosures.

Next steps, in order:

1. Verify at the primary source first, before any figure enters a draft: the Bajaj Finance Q4 FY26 and Q1 FY27 earnings-call transcripts and investor decks (71% and 72% self-service, Rs 2,500 crore, Rs 1,895 crore, 17-18% originations, the one-third voice cost, and the target cut from 800+ to 600+); the RBI FREE-AI report PDF (583 applications, the 15.6/11.8/13.7% split, 20.8% adoption, the 15% and 35% explainability and bias figures); the RBI Annual Report 2024-25 fraud tables (23,953 cases, Rs 36,014 crore); and the Sahamati FY26 report (Rs 3.82 lakh crore, 18.2% new-to-credit).

3. Do not cite the flagged items listed in the banking section (15-25% default-prediction and 30% default-reduction claim, the Accenture 65% claim, the Trak.in 1,500-agent claim) unless a primary source is found.
4. Read the HDFC, Axis and ICICI primary reports (IR26, the Q4FY26 deck, Form 6-K) and extract exact AI wording and numbers with page references, then confirm the Digital Lending Directions and KYC/V-CIP text on rbi.org.in and the DPDP timeline on MeitY.

6. If the student switches to a fallback, the first checks differ. For insurance, pull the IRDAI Annual Report PDF, the NHA annual report and the IRDAI AI working-group report (due about September 2026, status unchecked). For e-commerce, open the Business Standard Myntra and Flipkart pieces manually, read the Meesho RHP, and check whether Flipkart has filed a DRHP.

## Conclusion

The comparison shows that the "best" industry for this project is not the one with the most AI activity or the most famous cases. E-commerce has plenty of both, and its evidence is the least checkable. The deciding factor is whether the project can attach a source, a year and a number to each stage, and banking's quarterly management disclosures and regulator survey make that possible more often than the others. Even so, none of the three can support causal claims about AI impact. The report should state this openly as a limitation, and the gap analysis should be written as testable hypotheses, not findings.

A second implication is that the narrowed scope matters more than the industry. Every note concluded that a full end-to-end study is infeasible in 80 hours, and each narrowed scope lands where a public data hook meets a documented regulatory event: RBI's FREE-AI framework and loan-fraud value in banking, IRDAI's AI working group and NHCX in insurance, DPDP and dark-pattern rules in e-commerce. The next 80 hours are therefore best spent verifying the few numbers that carry the recommendation, and not on collecting more of them.
